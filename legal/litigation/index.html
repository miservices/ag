Litigation and representation

How the State is represented in civil matters, and how to serve the Department.

Serve the DepartmentAll legal services

Official capacity suits must be served on the Attorney General

By law, a suit against the State or an official in their official capacity must be served directly on the Attorney General. Serving through MiBRIDGES satisfies that requirement.

Civil matters for the State

The Office of the Solicitor General handles all civil matters for the State of Michigan. It represents the State in civil litigation under the direction of the Attorney General.

Official capacity suits

A suit against an official in their official capacity is legally a suit against the government office or agency itself. In these cases the Attorney General defends the government's interests and positions.

Under recent law, this generally extends to county and municipal employees as well as State employees.

Individual capacity suits

When an employee is sued in their individual capacity, representation is discretionary. It requires both of the following:

  1. A written request for representation.
  2. A determination from the Attorney General or the Solicitor General that the representation serves the interests of the State.

What to put in your request

  • Your name, position and employer
  • The court and case number, if there is one
  • A description of the claim against you
  • Why representing you serves the interests of the State

Send a written request

Serving the Department

Official capacity suits are required by law to be served directly on the Attorney General. That requirement is met by serving through MiBRIDGES, the Michigan Bureaucratic Routing and Intergovernmental Data Exchange System.

Service through MiBRIDGES is legally sufficient, even though the papers are usually received by staff below the Attorney General or the Solicitor General themselves.
